## Scope and frozen screen

B4-06 requires I-T2/I-T3/I-T6, exact material accounting, and imported-kit, mine-loss, fuel-loss, captured-forge, mixed-wear, and upgrade cases ([25 §14](../25-iron-age.md#14-proposed-m11-acceptance-series-b4-xx)). The fixed I-T6 cutoffs remain unchanged: at least 12 positive planned-charcoal demand-seasons, shortfall rate at most 5%, and unmet charcoal at most 1% of planned volume. The I-T2 target remains at least 70% of tool workers equipped T11+ within 12 seasons of a sustainable route.

The harness records actual settlement tool workers as adults with positive `toolWork` during the season, after that season's kit issue. The I-T2 diagnostic starts a settlement window at its first T11/T12 kit issue or sister delivery and ends after 12 complete seasons. It follows each kit tier backward through whole-kit transfers and applies fuel cutoffs only to sites that produced the supplied tier during that window. A relay-only or stock-only sender, missing source snapshot, ownership/liveness change, or fewer than 12 pooled producer-site demand-seasons censors the route instead of allowing unrelated fuel demand to qualify it. The shortfall rate and unmet/planned volume are pooled across the proven producer-site seasons, with the unchanged minimum of 12 demand-seasons. Coverage and its age bands are still reported for censored windows. The report separates MET-09 access season, hub assignment and facilities, site and hub wood factors, local kit production, exact sister-settlement kit deliveries and their sources, issuance, and T11-to-T12 upgrades. Captured sites are censored for the I-T2 denominator because ownership changes within the measurement window.

The `iron-fuel --it3-wear` trace reports live T11/T12 holders in fixed bins of remaining service as a fraction of that tier's service life. Its mixed-tier scenario starts with T11, grants MET-10 after 120 seasons, and records the distribution at route+12, MET-10+12, and run end. The trace is read-only and does not add state to simulation checksums or snapshots.

## Assigned-hub fuel-access candidate and import feasibility

A matched pair of single-seed diagnostics kept the accepted physical and siting radii at 40/36. The treatment enabled a candidate in which a settlement may use forest tiles in its directly assigned fuel hub's catchment, charging member-to-hub plus hub-to-forest distance and debiting each live tile once; the control omitted that toggle. Both used the same source tree, content pack and seed `20261965`, and are not acceptance samples. Treatment command: `iron-match --pack /tmp/civsim-b406-acceptance-final.pack --seed 20261965 --minutes 86 --iron-hub-fuel-sharing --it6-supply-diagnostic`. Control command is the same without `--iron-hub-fuel-sharing`. The toggle is diagnostic-only and remains off by default.

The candidate improved c2's post-MET-09 fuel result from the baseline's 41.73% short and 39.86% unmet to 16.50% short and 18.07% unmet (50/303 demand-seasons; 75.66/418.64 L). Both unchanged cutoffs still fail. c0 also failed the volume limit at 2.44% unmet (13.68/560.93 L), even though its shortage rate was 2.50%; c1 and c3 passed both fuel cutoffs. I-T2 reported 29 pass, 1 fail and 74 censored; s32 ended at 6/10 toolworkers equipped (60%, below 70%). In that route window s14 made 44.06 T11 kits and sent 52; s32 received 9, sent 5, issued 9 and retired 1. A same-seed seasonal relocation trace now identifies the endpoint change: in season 293 s32 fell from 53 to 33 people and from 11 to 6 T11 holders while sending five kits to new s92 (0 to 22 people, five kits received). At the same event s65 fell from 30 to 18 people and sent two kits to new s93 (0 to 12 people, two received). These match the settlement-split relocation accounting. s32 therefore retained six kits for ten tool workers and failed its 70% local coverage screen; s92 later had 4/4 coverage but was censored at only two fuel-demand seasons, and s93 was censored because its received kits' source did not produce in its window. This is a real residual-parent coverage failure under the current per-settlement rule, not a whole-world kit loss. It points to post-fission replenishment or a formally reviewed fission-window rule; the I-T2 target is unchanged. The candidate's maximum mature rolling work share was 10.32%, and whole-match forest, local-wood and kit residuals were zero.

The pair added a read-only cross-civilization inventory feasibility trace. It counts only stored wood above the 12 L reserve and the current generic four-season wood need, and captures contact, nonnegative relations, bilateral peace, path reachability and route duration at the shortage season. It does not model a Wood Compact, standing-forest harvest by the seller, a finite cargo limit, third-party route blocks or shipment labor. The aggregate counts all reachable foreign settlement inventories once; an existing Compact would select one seller hub and one buyer hub per counterpart and good, so the aggregate is an optimistic ceiling above that mechanism. In the treatment, at season 293 c2/s15 needed 9.92 L of wood to cover that season's 1.65 L charcoal miss; the best listed foreign stock was 13.19 L at c1/s67, five travel seasons away. By season 306, six c2 sites needed 89.40 L to cover their current misses, while only two deduplicated event-time reachable foreign settlement inventories offered at most 23.35 L (26.12%) and the shortest route was four seasons. In the no-sharing control, at season 338 six c2 sites needed 21.52 L and three deduplicated event-time reachable foreign inventories held at most 10.62 L (49.35%), also at a four-season minimum route. The affected c2 sites had no estimated spare Iron ore stock under the same generic four-season reserve calculation. This shows some reachable stored wood exists, but not enough to cover the mature treatment shortage, and it does not support an immediate wood-for-ore exchange.

A second same-seed diagnostic applied greedy stored-inventory shipments only after observed charcoal was already unmet, debited seller surplus once, and delivered after captured route time. It is an optimistic stored-inventory counterfactual, not a formal maximum, physical trade implementation or acceptance result: it assumes delivered wood immediately covers observed unmet charcoal and omits labor, cargo caps, payment, losses, third-party blocks, standing-forest harvest, changing routes and compact hub limits. Its reconstructed control baseline exactly matched the frozen c2 result (159/381 short; 242.97/609.56 L unmet/planned). The counterfactual changed that to 156/381 and 232.14/609.56 L (38.08%), still far above both I-T6 cutoffs. In the hub-sharing treatment, c2 changed from 50/303 and 75.66/418.64 L (18.07%) to 48/303 and 68.35/418.64 L (16.33%); c0 remained over the volume cutoff at 9.15/560.93 L (1.63%). This reactive stored-inventory model therefore does not close the fuel failures. It does not test whether a pre-positioned, forest-harvest-based exporter could supply enough fuel before shortage.

**Decision:** stop the domestic sharing candidate as a complete fix; it materially reduces but does not clear the approved fuel failures, and one I-T2 endpoint still misses coverage. Stop the reactive stored-inventory import model as a sufficient fix: it changes the c2 shortage rate and unmet volume only marginally and is not a real trade system. Keep a Wood Compact and wood-for-ore exchange shelved as gameplay changes. Revisit a pre-positioned, forest-harvest-based import only if a physical supplier, sustainable surplus, route capacity and delivered volume can be demonstrated; the current c2 stock trace does not demonstrate those, and c2 has no estimated spare Iron ore to export. For the sharing treatment alone, reducing its c2 unmet volume from 75.66 L to the 1% allowance (4.19 L) requires about **429 L of total delivered wood-equivalent** at the 6:1 recipe rate; the diagnostic already used 43.83 L, leaving about 385 L beyond those modeled deliveries. Do not claim I-T6 closure. The approved cutoffs remain unchanged. Any future candidate requires a fresh preregistration before acceptance.
