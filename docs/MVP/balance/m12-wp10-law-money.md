# M12 WP-E5-7 (first slice): law and money

**Status:** One existing typed rule is verified through the live economy and its focused test passes in Release: GOV-11 adds one point of administrative span when complete in Fief. A follow-up feasibility audit traced TRD-08 through caravan dispatch and settlement. It did not add a credit rule: the current ledger has no bounded repayment rule that can be applied without inventing economic behavior. This is not completion or balance signoff for law and money. GOV-11 and all other E5 rows remain draft; every numeric behavior below remains provisional.

## Implemented slice

- GOV-11 already contains an `admin span: 1` effect. The generic admin-span path sums completed effects, so the Fief economy receives the additional span without new saved state or a second rule implementation.
- `FiefLawMoneyTests` compares the same seeded Fief fixture with and without GOV-11 and checks the +1. ## Deferred

- GOV-11's `city_growth ×1.1` applies to chartered towns, but the economy has no chartered-town state or selection rule. Applying it to all towns would change the proposal.
- GOV-10's dispute/crime event multiplier has no crime/dispute event model.
- **TRD-08 credit feasibility audit (2026-11-14): no runtime change.** Trade does already escrow an existing seller good in the persisted caravan `Expedition.Cargo`; on arrival, `ArriveCaravan` takes existing payment goods from the buyer, credits them to the seller, and delivers only the matching fraction of cargo. This conserves goods for current barter. A credit bill could record that unpaid amount on the persisted `Compact`, but no existing rule defines which future resource services the claim, when collection occurs, what food reserve is protected, or what happens when a route ends before repayment. Scarcity prices also vary by settlement stock, so revaluing an outstanding bill later would not preserve the original claim. Adding a balance field alone would defer accounting without settling a resource claim. We therefore do not approximate money creation or an arbitrary repayment schedule.
- **Concrete seam required before implementation:** define a Fief + completed-TRD-08 eligibility check; fix the bill's unit and origination value; choose an existing repayment good and a rule that protects its forecast need; define partial payment, route termination, and debtor/seller settlement-loss behavior; then append the liability to the Compact snapshot/hash schema. Required regression coverage must show the shipped good moves from seller escrow to buyer, each repayment moves existing stock from debtor to creditor, unpaid balances reconcile, Fief-with-TRD-08 is the only active case, and Iron/Bronze checksums and behavior stay unchanged. Snapshot/hash continuation must cover an outstanding bill and partial repayment. Until those rules are specified, existing immediate barter remains the only supported path.
- **Decision comparison — non-minting in-kind credit candidates (design only):** each candidate records a claim on goods already held or later produced; none creates a spendable token or changes the existing barter ledger. Current trade already supports escrow of seller cargo and transfer of buyer payment on arrival. These are candidate rule shapes, not selected policy or implemented behavior.

  | Candidate | Origination | Repayment basis and partial payment | Food-reserve priority | Route termination and default |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **A. Same-good quantity claim** | At arrival, deliver the agreed cargo while recording the unpaid quantity of the buyer's agreed payment good as a claim. The claim unit is that good's existing unit, fixed at origination. | Collect that same good from the debtor's available stock and transfer it to the creditor; reduce the outstanding quantity only by the amount actually transferred. No later market-price conversion. | Apply a chosen reserve rule to the repayment good before collection. Food claims require an explicit protected food need/reserve; other goods need a stated priority against food and other obligations. | A route ending does not erase the recorded claim. Decide whether the creditor can collect at the debtor settlement, whether collection is suspended if settlements become unreachable, and whether settlement loss cancels, transfers, or leaves the claim outstanding. Define default as the chosen terminal outcome after the chosen trigger; no trigger is assumed here. |
  | **B. Origination-valued claim, paid in eligible goods** | Record the unpaid payment value at origination using the existing barter exchange basis, so later stock-driven price changes do not revalue the bill. | Accept one or more explicitly eligible existing goods, valued using the locked origination basis; each partial transfer reduces the balance by its origination value. This requires fixing the eligible goods and conversion/rounding convention. | Reserve protection is evaluated before converting eligible debtor stocks into payment value. The policy must specify whether food can ever be diverted and which forecast need or reserve takes precedence. | The value claim persists independently of the route. Decide local collection access, treatment of a broken route, and settlement-loss effects. Default needs a specified trigger and disposition of the remaining value; no automatic write-off or seizure is presumed. |
  | **C. Scheduled delivery claim in the cargo good** | Record a fixed quantity of the cargo good owed by the buyer, using the quantity delivered on credit as the claim unit. | Transfer that same good from debtor to creditor when available; partial delivery reduces the remaining quantity. This is simple to audit but only works when the debtor can produce or obtain that good. | If the claimed good is food, collection must preserve the selected food reserve; if it is not food, specify its priority relative to food production and other uses. | The claim persists if the route ends, but collection may be impossible without access to the debtor's settlement. Decide whether it waits, is settled locally, follows either party after settlement loss, or is cancelled; define the default trigger and remaining-claim outcome. |

  **Minimum user choices required before safe implementation:** (1) choose A, B, or C; (2) define the origination amount and its unit/value rule, including eligible repayment good(s) and any conversion/rounding rule; (3) define when repayment is attempted and how partial transfers reduce the claim; (4) define the food-reserve/need rule and repayment priority; and (5) define route-termination, settlement-loss, and default trigger/outcome rules. Until these choices are made, TRD-08 credit remains unimplemented and immediate barter is the only supported path.
